# Known limitations

## Release and platform

* **The Windows executable is built and self-tested automatically, not by hand.** Releases come from a GitHub
  Actions workflow on a Windows Server 2022 runner, which runs the tests, builds with PyInstaller and runs the
  built executable's self-test. It has not yet been tried by a person on a desktop Windows 10/11 PC, so display
  scaling and font rendering on your screen are unverified.
* The executable is not code-signed, so SmartScreen may warn on first run.
* Dependencies are pinned and verified for 64-bit Python 3.12 on Windows. Other Python versions are not
  supported by the build script.
* The interface is English only; numbers use English (United States) formatting.

## Data and realism

* Every figure, company, event, tax setting and benchmark is **fictional**. Nothing should be quoted as real
  industry data.
* Tax is simplified: one corporate income-tax rate on profit before tax with loss carry-forward; no deferred tax,
  zakat, withholding tax or tax-depreciation schedules other than straight-line in the investment cases. VAT is
  output VAT on revenue only; input VAT on purchases is not modelled.
* Interest is calculated on opening balances (no intra-month circularity).
* Demand is a parametric model (seasonality, day of week, events, growth, constant price elasticity, seeded noise).
  There is no competitor set, no length-of-stay network optimisation and no booking-curve simulation per arrival
  date; pace curves are illustrative.
* Overbooking walks use a normal approximation of arrival uncertainty.
* Group blocks are assumed to be contracted before transient demand books and are not subject to cancellation.
* The 36-month horizon is fixed; investment cases extend year 3 at a stated growth rate beyond it.
* USD is a fixed fictional conversion for display; there is no FX risk modelling.
* The Aurelia Oasis Resort is modelled with the same engine and assumptions structure as the Grand.

## Assessment

* Decision Room, capstone text answers and interpretation questions are graded by transparent **rules**
  (structure, numbers, drivers, required elements, keyword groups, length). They cannot judge prose quality,
  tone or originality like a person, and a well-reasoned answer phrased unusually can score lower than it deserves.
  The feedback says what the rubric looked for.
* Competency levels summarise evidence recorded in this app only. They are not an accredited qualification,
  certification or guarantee of employment.
* The coach is deterministic and offline. It recognises the mistake patterns it was written for (signs, scale
  errors, specific topic mistakes) and otherwise gives general hints and the worked method.

## Excel

* The grader's calculator supports 34 Excel functions (listed in the Excel Lab). Other functions are graded on the
  value Excel saved; files saved without stored results (some online or third-party editors) cannot be checked for
  those cells.
* Dynamic arrays, structured table references, named ranges, macros, data tables and external links are not
  evaluated.
* Only workbooks generated by the Excel Lab can be graded.
* Exports to Excel contain values (snapshots), not live formulas.

## Application

* Single user per Windows account; no multi-user or network database.
* PDF reports use Qt's rich-text renderer: layout is simple (A4, tables, headings) rather than typeset.
* Monte Carlo runs the full engine per iteration; large iteration counts take noticeable time.
* There is no automatic update mechanism (by design: the app makes no network connections).

## Season mode

* Each month of a season is simulated from the Base case opening balance sheet. The season tracks cash itself as
  opening cash plus each played month's net cash flow, so working-capital timing of earlier decisions is not
  carried into later months.
* Briefing cards come from a fixed, fictional set (8 market, 6 decision and 5 issue cards) and their effects are
  training assumptions, not estimates of how real guests, accounts or suppliers behave.
